Common questions

How long do I have to send a cheque bounce notice?

Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, the notice must be sent within 30 days of receiving the bank's cheque return memo. The person who issued the cheque then has 15 days to pay. If they don't, a complaint can be filed within one month after those 15 days end.

What documents do I need?

Keep the original bounced cheque, the bank's return memo, and anything that shows the money was owed, such as invoices, messages, an agreement or bank transfers. The law presumes a cheque was given for a legally enforceable debt, but the other side can challenge that, so records help.

Is there a time limit to recover unpaid money?

For most money recovery claims the general limitation period is three years under the Limitation Act, 1963, but it depends on the type of claim. It's best to act early.

I received a legal notice. Do I have to reply?

It's usually wise to reply within the time stated in the notice. A clear reply puts your side on record, and that can matter if the dispute goes to court.
