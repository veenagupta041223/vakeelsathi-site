Mutual Consent Divorce in India: The Process

Last Updated: October 9, 2026

Mutual consent divorce is usually the quickest and least stressful way to end a marriage in India, because both spouses agree to it and to the terms. This guide explains the process under Section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 and other personal laws have similar provisions.

Who can apply

  • Both spouses must genuinely agree to the divorce
  • They must have lived separately for at least one year before filing. "Separately" means not living as husband and wife, even if under the same roof in some cases
  • They should agree on issues such as alimony or maintenance, custody of children, and return of belongings

Step 1: First motion

Both spouses file a joint petition before the family court, stating that they have lived apart for a year and have agreed to divorce. The court records their statements.

Step 2: Cooling-off period

The law provides a six-month period after the first motion, giving the couple time to reconsider. In Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017), the Supreme Court held that a family court can waive this period in suitable cases, for example where the couple has been separated for a long time and settled all issues.

Step 3: Second motion and decree

After the cooling-off period, or once it is waived, both spouses confirm that they still want the divorce. The second motion should be made within 18 months of the first. If the court is satisfied, it grants the divorce decree.

Documents usually needed

  • Marriage certificate, or wedding photographs and invitation
  • Address and identity proof of both spouses
  • Proof of separate residence for at least one year, where available
  • Details of the settlement on alimony, custody and belongings

Things to keep in mind

  • Either spouse can withdraw consent before the decree is passed
  • Put the settlement terms in writing so that both sides are clear
  • Where children are involved, the court will look at their welfare when approving custody arrangements
